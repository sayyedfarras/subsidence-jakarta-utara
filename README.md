# Jakarta Utara Land Subsidence Analysis Using InSAR, DEMNAS, and Groundwater Data

## Overview
This project was developed as part of a computational geology study focusing on land subsidence in North Jakarta, Indonesia. Land subsidence has become one of the most significant geohazards affecting the region, causing increased flood risk, infrastructure damage, and environmental degradation. The project utilizes geospatial data processing and analysis in Google Colab to investigate the spatial distribution of subsidence, identify high-risk areas, evaluate relationships with land use, and assess the influence of groundwater decline.

The workflow integrates multiple datasets, including InSAR-derived vertical displacement data, DEMNAS elevation data, RBI land-use maps, administrative boundary shapefiles, and groundwater monitoring well records. All analyses are performed using Python-based geospatial libraries within Google Colab.

---

## Objectives
The main objectives of this project are:
* Map the spatial distribution of land subsidence in North Jakarta.
* Identify areas with the highest subsidence risk.
* Compare subsidence characteristics across different land-use categories.
* Analyze subsidence statistics at the district (*kecamatan*) level.
* Predict future subsidence patterns based on current trends.
* Evaluate groundwater level decline.
* Investigate the relationship between groundwater depletion and land subsidence.

## Data Sources
> ⚠️ **Note on Datasets:** Due to file size limitations on GitHub (>25MB), the raw raster datasets (`.tif`) for **DEMNAS** and **InSAR** are processed locally and are not included directly in this repository. All processed outputs and figures remain fully accessible.

1. **InSAR Vertical Displacement Raster** (Processed Locally)
   Primary dataset representing land subsidence rates derived from satellite-based Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar (InSAR).
2. **DEMNAS** (Processed Locally)
   Digital Elevation Model Nasional (DEMNAS) used to incorporate topographic conditions into subsidence risk assessment.
3. **RBI Land Use Data**  
   Indonesian topographic map data (*Rupa Bumi Indonesia*) used to classify land use and compare subsidence among different land-use categories.
4. **Administrative Boundary Shapefile**  
   District (*kecamatan*) boundaries used for spatial aggregation and statistical analysis.
5. **Groundwater Monitoring Well Data**  
   Time-series groundwater level measurements (*sumur pantau*) used to assess groundwater decline and its correlation with land subsidence.
---

## Project Outputs
* **Output 1 — Land Subsidence Map**  
  A spatial visualization of land subsidence across North Jakarta derived from InSAR data. The map highlights areas experiencing significant ground deformation and provides a foundation for subsequent analyses.
* **Output 2 — Subsidence Risk Heatmap**  
  A risk assessment map generated by combining InSAR-derived subsidence rates with DEMNAS elevation data. Areas are classified into different risk levels based on their susceptibility to land subsidence impacts.
* **Output 3 — Land Use Subsidence Analysis**  
  A boxplot-based comparison of land subsidence values across different land-use categories, including industrial areas, residential zones, agricultural land, and other land-cover types derived from RBI data.
* **Output 4 — District-Level Statistics**  
  Statistical summaries of subsidence for each district (*kecamatan*), including mean, minimum, maximum, and variability values. Results are visualized through maps and charts to identify the most affected districts.
* **Output 5 — 10-Year Subsidence Prediction**  
  A predictive model estimating cumulative land subsidence over the next ten years under the assumption that current subsidence rates remain constant.
* **Output 6 — Groundwater Level Decline Analysis**  
  An assessment of groundwater level changes based on monitoring well records. The analysis quantifies groundwater decline across observation periods and identifies areas experiencing significant depletion.
* **Output 7 — Groundwater–Subsidence Relationship**  
  An exploratory analysis examining the relationship between groundwater level decline and land subsidence. The output helps evaluate whether groundwater extraction contributes to observed subsidence patterns.

## Methodology
The project follows a geospatial analysis workflow consisting of:
1. Data acquisition and preprocessing.
2. Raster clipping to North Jakarta administrative boundaries.
3. Land subsidence visualization using InSAR data.
4. Risk assessment through weighted overlay analysis.
5. Spatial extraction of subsidence values by land-use category.
6. Statistical analysis by district boundaries.
7. Temporal projection of future subsidence.
8. Groundwater decline calculation.
9. Correlation analysis between groundwater depletion and land subsidence.

## Key Findings
The analysis demonstrates that land subsidence in North Jakarta is spatially heterogeneous, with several areas exhibiting significantly higher subsidence rates than others. The integration of elevation data improves risk identification, while land-use analysis provides insight into how subsidence varies among different surface utilization types. Groundwater data further support investigations into potential drivers of subsidence, particularly in heavily urbanized regions.
